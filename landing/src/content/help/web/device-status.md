# Device status

Every device on the Devices page — and every device listed on a partner's card
— carries a status tag. There are three, and they answer slightly different
questions: is this device still sending, and is it still set up at all?

## Online

The device uploaded a batch of activity within the last two hours.

This is the normal state for a device that's in use. A device that's switched
off, asleep, or offline for the night will fall out of it without anything
being wrong.

## Offline

The device is still set up, but its last upload was more than two hours ago.

Common and usually harmless: the computer or phone is off, sleeping, or has no
internet connection. It'll flip back to **Online** on its own once the device
uploads again — no action needed on your side.

Offline stops being routine when it lasts. A device that's offline for days
while the person is clearly using it is worth asking about: the app may have
been stopped, blocked from the network, or removed from startup.

The **(last seen)** time next to each device tells you which of those you're
looking at.

## Deactivated

Someone logged the device out. It is no longer being monitored, and it will not
come back on its own — the device has to be set up and signed in again.

This is the one status that always means a deliberate action was taken. Logging
out is not something that happens by accident or through a bad connection, so
a device that's gone from Online to Deactivated has been signed out by whoever
has access to it.

Both you and your partners are emailed when a device logs out, so this status
shouldn't be the first you hear of it. If you didn't expect it, that's worth a
conversation — and if it was you, just sign the device back in.

The device's existing logs stay where they are; deactivating does not delete
anything. Deleting a device from the Devices page is a separate action that
does remove its stored batches.
